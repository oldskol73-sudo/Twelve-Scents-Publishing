Reading the Word Together: A Family Devotional Guide

Five simple questions to ask after storytime that turn a picture book into a real conversation about faith.

By the Twelve Scents Team·May 19, 2026·4 min read
Family Devotional

We hear it often from parents: "My child loves the pictures, but I want the story to stick with them past bedtime." That's the exact gap a devotional conversation is built to close. A storybook plants the seed. A few honest questions afterward are what help it take root.

You don't need a curriculum or a lesson plan. You need about five minutes, a comfortable spot on the couch, and permission to let your child's answers surprise you.

Five questions to ask after any Twelve Scents story

These work after Abraham, after Sarah, after any hero in the collection — swap in the character's name and let the conversation go wherever your child takes it.

1. What was the hardest part for [hero] to do? This teaches children to notice obedience as a choice, not a given.
2. Was [hero] scared? How do you know? Faith and fear are allowed to coexist — that's a lesson worth naming out loud.
3. What would you have done? Let them sit in the tension honestly, even if their answer isn't the "right" one.
4. Who helped [hero] along the way? No hero in Scripture walks alone, and neither does your child.
5. What's one thing you want to remember from this story tomorrow? This turns a bedtime book into something they carry into the next day.

"And these words, which I command thee this day, shall be in thine heart: and thou shalt teach them diligently unto thy children." Deuteronomy 6:6–7 · KJV 1611

Consistency matters more than perfection

You will not get through all five questions every night. Some nights you'll get one, mumbled, half-asleep. That's fine. The goal isn't a flawless devotional routine — it's a habit of turning toward Scripture together often enough that your child starts turning toward it on their own.

Every Twelve Scents storybook is written with this exact conversation in mind — which is part of why we treat these covers as more than picture books. They're an invitation into your family's own reading ministry.
