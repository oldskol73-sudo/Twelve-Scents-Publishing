Meet Abraham: The Father Who Believed God

Long before he was a legend, Abraham was a man asked to trust a promise he could not yet see.

By the Twelve Scents Team·July 14, 2026·4 min read
Book One · Pre-Sale

Every great story has to start somewhere, and for Twelve Scents Publishing, it started with a father. Not a warrior, not a king — a man who packed up everything he knew and walked toward a country he had never seen, because a voice he trusted told him to go. That's Abraham. And that's exactly why he's Book One.

We didn't choose Abraham because his story is easy. We chose him because it isn't. He waited decades for a promised son. He made mistakes along the way — plenty of them. And still, Scripture says he "believed in the LORD; and he counted it to him for righteousness" (Genesis 15:6, KJV). That single verse became the compass for our entire publishing house: faith isn't the absence of doubt or delay. It's choosing to keep walking anyway.

Why brown children need to meet him first

For a long time, the picture books on our shelves quietly taught brown children that biblical heroes belonged to someone else — pale illustrations standing in for people who, historically and geographically, looked nothing like that. Meet Abraham was our answer: a father from the land of Ur, drawn with the dignity and likeness his story deserves, so that a child flipping through these pages sees not a stranger, but an ancestor.

The illustrations lean into warm, sun-worn tones — desert light, woven fabric, weathered hands — because we wanted every spread to feel less like a costume and more like a memory. Our illustrator spent weeks referencing the geography of the ancient Near East before a single line was drawn.

"Get thee out of thy country, and from thy kindred, and from thy father's house, unto a land that I will shew thee." Genesis 12:1 · KJV 1611

What we hope children carry with them

We didn't write Meet Abraham to teach children a Bible fact. We wrote it so a child closes the book believing something bigger: that obedience — even uncertain, even unfinished — is still faith. That you can be afraid and still go. That the promise doesn't have to make sense yet for it to be worth trusting.

Abraham's story doesn't end in this book. It echoes through Isaac, through Jacob, through every hero still to come in the Men of Valor series. He is the root the rest of the family tree grows from — which is exactly why he had to be first.
