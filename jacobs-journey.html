Jacob's Journey: Wrestling With Purpose

Jacob's story is messy, human, and ultimately redemptive — exactly why it resonates so deeply with kids who are still figuring themselves out.

By the Twelve Scents Team·June 20, 2026·4 min read
Book Three · Pre-Sale

If Abraham is faith and Isaac is inheritance, Jacob is something children need just as much: permission to be a work in progress. Jacob schemes. He runs from his mistakes. He gets tricked right back. And still, somehow, he becomes Israel — the name that an entire nation would eventually carry.

We wanted a hero whose flaws are on the page, not edited out for the sake of a tidy moral. Kids already know they're not perfect. What they need is proof that God isn't finished with someone just because chapter one was a mess.

The famous wrestling match

The centerpiece of this book is the night Jacob wrestles a stranger until daybreak and refuses to let go without a blessing. It's a strange, physical, almost uncomfortable scene to put in a children's book — and that's exactly why we kept it in. Purpose is rarely handed over easily. Sometimes you have to hold on through the dark, limping, and still not quit.

Our illustrator treated this scene with real weight: dawn breaking over a riverbank, two figures locked together, and Jacob's face caught between exhaustion and stubborn hope.

"And he said, Thy name shall be called no more Jacob, but Israel: for as a prince hast thou power with God and with men, and hast prevailed." Genesis 32:28 · KJV 1611

A new name, not a clean slate

Jacob doesn't get a spotless past erased — he gets a new name to grow into. That distinction matters. Children carrying guilt, comparison, or the sense that they've already messed up too badly need to hear that redemption doesn't require pretending the mess never happened.

This is the book we hand to the child who feels most like the underdog in their own story — because Jacob was too, right up until he wasn't.
