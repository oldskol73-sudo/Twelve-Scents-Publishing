Behind the Scents

Why We Chose the 1611 King James Bible as Our Foundation

Every hero we publish is drawn from Scripture, not sentiment. Here's why the 1611 KJV anchors every book we write.

By the Twelve Scents Team·May 30, 2026·6 min read
Our Foundation

People sometimes ask us why we're so specific about our source text. It would be easier, faster even, to write loose retellings and call them "Bible-inspired." We chose not to. Every hero in the Twelve Scents catalog — Abraham, Sarah, Isaac, Jacob, and every Daughter of Scripture — is drawn directly from the pages of the 1611 King James Bible, depicted according to the lands they walked and the lineage they carried.

That's not a stylistic preference. It's the whole point of the company.

Restoring the record, not adding representation

We did not set out to insert brown children into Scripture for the sake of representation. We set out to restore the record. The heroes of the Bible were brown-skinned men and women who lived, prayed, and obeyed in the actual geography of the ancient Near East and North Africa — and for far too long, illustrated children's Bibles have quietly erased that fact.

Anchoring every book in the 1611 KJV text gives us a fixed, defensible standard. When we illustrate Abraham's journey or Sarah's laughter, we aren't guessing at what "feels" biblical. We're depicting what Scripture actually describes, in the land it actually happened in.

"These are not stories. This is Scripture — the covenant inheritance of children who descend from the very lands where God spoke, where prophets walked, where the Messiah was born." Twelve Scents Publishing, Our Story

Why the diaspora carries the seed

We publish for the diaspora because the diaspora carries the seed. Every book is crafted to build character — courage, obedience, faith, forgiveness — not through sentiment, but through the weight of truth, and not through apology, but through the authority of the Word.

The shelves were empty of heroes who looked like the children reading them. We are filling them with the truth — one Scripture-rooted, historically grounded storybook at a time.
